#### **Activity 1: User Journey Map** 

* **Introduction: “**First, we want to hear about a typical trip you take. We want to know what you think and feel before you leave, while you are on the road, and after you get home.”

* **Goal**: To map a user's cognitive and emotional experience when a routine journey is unexpectedly broken to identify specific moments where the Adaptive Navigation System must intervene.


| Stage | Challenge or Problem Faced | What are you thinking feeling? | What action do you take? |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1\. Before Leaving** |  |  |  |
| **2\. On the Road** |  |  |  |
| **3\. After the Journey** |  |  |  |

**Questions:**

* When you felt \[mention emotion they listed\], what was happening around you exactly?  
* Is there a specific part of this journey that you worry about before you even leave the house? Why do you worry about those issues?  
* What measures do you take when you realize the journey isn't going as planned?  
* If you had to pick the difficult moment out of this whole trip, which one would it be?  
* What usually helps you feel calm or safe again after a problem happens?  
* Whom do you usually reach out for help? Why?


  





**Activity 2: The "Magic Button"** 

**Introduction**: “Next, we will give you a 'Magic Button.' We want you to imagine that if you were in a scary or confusing situation, this button could do one thing to help you. You get to decide what that is.”

**Goal:** Identifies the single, most critical action needed during a crisis (e.g., stuck at a bus stop

* Give the user a card representing a single button, explaining that this button will do the most important function during a crisis time that the user designs it to do.  
* Present a scenario based on disability, and ask “In this situation, what do you want this emergency button to do?” (Explain with pictures, text, or drawing wherever necessary)  
* Ask the participant to write, draw/use pictorial scenarios or orally explain  
* Ask why that action is the most important to them. (Can be written, drawn, orally explained)

**Possible scenarios to present:** 

**“What should the magic button do when…”:**

* You have to walk through a poorly lit area. You notice a group of people loitering ahead, and there is no one else around to help if something goes wrong  
* You are on a bus, but because of a sudden protest near Shahbag, the driver suddenly turns into a narrow side street you’ve never seen before. You realize you are moving further away from your destination and don't know where the next safe stop is  
* You are at the Farmgate footbridge area during rush hour. The noise from the traffic, the shouting of bus 'helpers,' and the massive crowd pushing past you makes you feel dizzy and completely lose your sense of direction.  
* You feel fatigued, and your body starts aching to an alarming degree  
* **For the Blind:** "A bus pulls up at the Azimpur stop, but three different buses are all honking at once. You can’t hear the specific engine sound of your regular bus or find the door in the chaos."  
* **For the Mute:** "You are inside a crowded 'Local Bus' and the helper is about to close the gate before you can signal that you need to get off at the next signal."  
* **For the Deaf:** "You are walking through a busy market when you see everyone suddenly looking behind them and running, but you didn't hear the siren or the warning shout.


**Questions (repeat for all scenarios):**

* In that situation, what is the very first thing you need to know or do?  
* Why did you choose that specific action for your button?  
* Who would you want to receive this information? A person you know, or someone nearby?  
* How would pressing this button change how you feel in that moment?  
* Is there anything you *wouldn't* want the button to do in this scenario?

**Activity 3: Build Your Assistant**

**Goal:** Defines the key features and communication style of the Adaptive Navigation System.

* **The Activity:**  
  "Pick the 2 most important cards/choices from each category. Glue/Write them onto this cardboard phone." ***Alternatively*****:** Participants can choose to draw or have researchers draw for each category.

**Probing Questions**:

* You picked \[Choice A\] and \[Choice B\], what makes these more helpful than the others?
