ANT · Spoken control reference

ANT Voice Phrasebook

Every function and every setting you can reach by speaking, in Bangla and English. These are matched on the phone itself — no model call, no network wait, so they work instantly and offline. Anything not on this list still works; it just goes to the assistant and takes a moment longer. Nothing here is a password: the app matches the words that carry the meaning and ignores the rest, so say it however comes naturally.

Start talking
Say “Hey Jarvis”, or tap the mic. Both open the same listener.
Two buzzes means go
The phone buzzes twice, then waits a beat, then listens. Speak after the second buzz.
You'll hear it back
Names, numbers and addresses are read back before anything is saved.
Say it your way
These are examples, not a script. Word order, politeness and filler are all handled.

While you're walking

You don't have to hold the phone or keep the screen on.

What it tells you

Spoken, and backed by vibration you can feel through a pocket.

You'll hear
  • The turn 200 metres ahead
  • Again at 50 metres
  • Again at 25 metres
  • "Now, turn left"
  • A warning before a reported hazard on your path
  • "You've gone off the route" if you leave it
  • "You've arrived"
You'll feel
  • One pattern for a turn coming up
  • A stronger one for turn now
  • A distinct one for a hazard warning
  • Two firm buzzes when it wants you to speak
  • Three light taps when a message is about to send
If you drift off the route it re-plans from where you actually are — you don't need to walk back. Routes avoid areas scored as higher risk for the time of day, so a night route may differ from the same trip in daylight.

What deliberately does nothing

Questions are recognised as questions. Negatives are recognised as negatives.

How long will it take?Same — it's an enquiry.
Is it safe around here?Answered as a question.

These guards exist because a wrong action costs far more than a missed one. Starting to walk someone across Dhaka because they wondered aloud about a place, or filing a hazard that closes a road for every other user because someone asked a question, are both failures a person who can't see the screen has no easy way to notice or undo.

Saying it your own way

Matching is on meaning-carrying words, not on whole phrases.

Every phrase in this document is an example of a shape, not a password. The app looks for the words that carry the meaning and ignores the rest, so please take me to Gulshan now, thanks and Gulshan, take me both work. Politeness, filler and hesitation words — um, okay, just, please, hey, so, well — are stripped before the destination is looked up.

Bangla is handled on its own terms rather than as English with different words. It puts the subject first and the verb last, so আমি গুলশান যেতে চাই is literally I Gulshan go want; the pronoun is removed so the place name reaches the map alone. Case endings attached to the noun — গুলশানে, ধানমন্ডিতে, অফিসের — are matched without being stripped, since the map handles them and cutting them wrongly would mangle real place names. Negation comes after the verb (যাব না), which is why it is checked as a whole word rather than searched for anywhere in the sentence.

English words inside a Bangla sentence are expected. Dhaka speech mixes constantly, and a Bangla recogniser writes English words in Bangla script — "send" comes back as সেন্ড. Those transliterations are matched too, not just the translations.

For an emergency, you do not have to say any of these words at all. The phrases above are matched instantly on the phone, with no network — but anything that sounds like you are in danger, hurt, trapped, lost or being followed reaches the assistant, which can raise the alarm on its own judgement.
